Before your test

A few simple steps help your results stay accurate.

  • Fasting tests: 8–12 hours with no food. Plain water is fine.
  • Medicines: take them as usual unless your doctor says otherwise. Tell us what you take.
  • Carry: your prescription, a photo ID, and any earlier reports.
  • Hydrate: drink water. It makes the draw easier.
  • Special tests: some (like cortisol or vitamin B12) need a timed sample. We’ll tell you on WhatsApp.

What your body runs on

Eating to improve your health

  • Half: vegetables and fruit. Many colours, including leafy greens, every day.
  • Quarter: whole grains. Jowar, ragi, millets, brown rice, whole wheat.
  • Quarter: protein. Dal, sprouts, eggs, fish, paneer, chicken, curd.
  • Plus: a little healthy fat (nuts, seeds, groundnut or mustard oil) and 2–3 litres of water a day, unless your doctor limits fluids.

High sugar or diabetes

Swap polished rice and maida for jowar, ragi, millets and brown rice. Add protein and fibre to every meal, walk 10 minutes after eating, and limit sweets and sugary drinks.

High cholesterol or heart health

Use less ghee and fried food. Choose oats, nuts, flaxseed, fruit, dal and fish. Cut packaged snacks and keep salt under 5 g a day.

Thyroid

Use iodised salt and eat enough protein. Take thyroid tablets on an empty stomach, and keep calcium and iron supplements about 4 hours apart from them.

Anaemia and low iron

Pair iron foods (ragi, beetroot, dates, greens, dal) with vitamin C (lemon, amla). Avoid tea or coffee with meals, since they block iron.

Bones and vitamin D

Get 15–20 minutes of morning sun, milk or curd daily, ragi and sesame. Add weight-bearing exercise like brisk walking.

Healthy weight

Eat more protein and vegetables and fewer refined carbs. Aim for slow loss of about 0.5 kg a week, not crash diets.

Daily habits that improve your results

  • Sleep 7–8 hours at regular times. Poor sleep raises cortisol and blood sugar.
  • Move: walk 30 minutes most days and do strength exercises twice a week.
  • Manage stress with breathing, hobbies and time outdoors.
  • Avoid tobacco and limit alcohol, packaged snacks and sugary drinks.
  • Test regularly. After 30, check sugar, lipids, thyroid, vitamin D and B12 once a year, or as your doctor advises.
  • Supplements: take them only after a test and doctor’s advice. Too much vitamin A, vitamin D or iron can be harmful.

This guide is for education only and does not replace a doctor. Seek urgent care for chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting or heavy bleeding.

Questions we hear every day

Is the blood draw painful?

You feel a quick pinch. Our phlebotomists are trained for children, seniors and people who are nervous about needles.

How will I get my report?

As a PDF on WhatsApp and email. You can also collect a printed copy at the lab.

Is my data private?

Yes. Reports are shared only with the number you booked from, and we never share results without your consent.

Can I reschedule or cancel?

Yes, just message us on WhatsApp at least two hours before your slot.

Do you accept doctor’s prescriptions and insurance?

We accept all prescriptions. Ask us on WhatsApp about insurance and corporate tie-ups.

What if a result is abnormal?

We flag it clearly and a lab doctor can explain it. Your own doctor should make treatment decisions.
